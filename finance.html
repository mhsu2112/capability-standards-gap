Finance: the Quiet Period and the architecture of co-regulation
Innovations, consortiums, incidents, and regulations plotted against total US financial sector assets (real 2025 dollars)
Gary Gorton's Slapped by the Invisible Hand (Oxford, 2010) identifies a sharp three-phase US banking history: a panic era with systemic crises in 1873, 1884, 1890, 1893, 1896, 1907, 1914, and 1930–33; a Quiet Period from 1934 to 2007 with no systemic banking panics, sustained by deposit insurance, Glass-Steagall, and a strong regulatory perimeter; and a return of panic beginning in 2007. What Gorton and Reinhart-Rogoff understate is finance's dense middle-governance layer: self-regulatory organizations recognized under the 1934 and 1938 Acts (NYSE, NASD/FINRA, MSRB), industry utilities built post-crisis (DTCC, NSCC, FICC), and standards bodies with quasi-public authority (FASB, ISDA, ARRC). Unlike communications, where industry rules emerged largely independent of government (IETF, W3C, 3GPP), finance's consortium layer is mostly co-regulatory: industry-built, statutorily recognized, and supervised by federal regulators. This is the model closest to what the AI memo proposes.
